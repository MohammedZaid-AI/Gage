# Sugarcane Nutrient/Fertilizer Management

## Summary
A 100 t/ha sugarcane crop removes substantial quantities of major and micro-nutrients from the soil (208 kg N, 53 kg P, 280 kg K, plus sulphur, iron, manganese and copper), requiring systematic replenishment. IISR documents zone-wise recommended fertilizer doses, split-application schedules, and organic/biofertilizer supplements that can reduce chemical fertilizer requirements by up to 25%.

## Main Information
- Nutrient removal by a 100 t/ha crop: 208 kg N, 53 kg P, 280 kg K, 30 kg S, 3.4 kg Fe, 1.2 kg Mn, 0.6 kg Cu.
- Regional nitrogen requirement differs sharply: **northern India needs about 150–180 kg N/ha, while southern India (including Karnataka) needs 250–350 kg N/ha.**
- If soil test values are unavailable, apply 60–80 kg P₂O₅ and 60 kg K₂O/ha in north India, versus **100 kg P₂O₅/ha and 80–120 kg K₂O/ha in southern India.**
- **Zone-wise recommended fertilizer dose relevant to Karnataka (Peninsular Zone)**: Plant crop — 250 kg N, 100 kg P₂O₅, 125 kg K₂O per hectare; Ratoon crop — 200 kg N, 60 kg P₂O₅, 60 kg K₂O per hectare.
- Application of well-decomposed FYM/compost at 10–15 t/ha, or composted press mud at 5 t/ha, is recommended as a base organic supplement.
- Soil application of Azotobacter or Azospirillum biofertilizer (10 kg/ha, CFU 10⁷–10⁹) in two equal splits at 30 and 60 days after planting gives a 25% saving in nitrogenous fertilizer requirement.
- Trash mulching in the ratoon crop significantly increases yield versus no mulching by conserving soil moisture, controlling weeds, and creating a favourable rhizo-microbiome for nutrient availability.
- Applying the recommended nitrogen dose in three to four splits (rather than a single dose) increases the number of millable canes and improves seed cane yield and quality; in some soils, an extra 25% nitrogen plus 25 kg K₂O/ha as an additional dose significantly raised seed cane yield.
- Incorporating sulphitated press mud at 4 t/ha combined with 75% of the recommended nitrogen dose achieves cane yield equal to the full recommended nitrogen dose alone — a 25% nitrogen saving.
- Sulphur should be applied at 40–60 kg/ha in sulphur-deficient soils, with gypsum identified as the most effective and economical source.
- Where micronutrient deficiency exists, apply per hectare: 25 kg Ferrous Sulphate, 20 kg Zinc Sulphate, 10 kg Manganese Sulphate, 10 kg Copper Sulphate, 2.5 kg Sodium Molybdate, and 5 kg Borax, mixed with well-decomposed manure/compost and applied as a basal dose.
- **Ratoon crop fertilizer schedule**: full phosphorus and potash plus one-third of nitrogen at ratoon initiation, well incorporated into soil; remaining nitrogen in two split doses at 45 and 90 days after ratooning. Ratoon nitrogen requirement is 25% higher than the plant crop.

## Important Recommendations
1. Karnataka farmers should apply substantially higher nitrogen (250–350 kg N/ha range for southern India) than the subtropical benchmark of 150–180 kg N/ha, reflecting the region's longer growing season and higher-yield potential.
2. Split nitrogen application into 3–4 doses rather than applying it all at planting, to align nutrient supply with crop demand across formative and grand-growth phases and to reduce leaching losses.
3. Combine biofertilizer treatment (Azotobacter/Azospirillum, Phosphobacteria) with chemical fertilizer to cut chemical N requirement by up to 25% — directly relevant for cost reduction advice to farmers.
4. In ratoon crops, apply 25% more nitrogen than for the plant crop, split as one-third at ratoon initiation and remaining two-thirds at 45 and 90 days after ratooning.

## Key Facts
- Southern India (incl. Karnataka) nitrogen requirement: 250–350 kg N/ha, versus 150–180 kg N/ha in northern India.
- Karnataka peninsular-zone recommended dose (plant crop): 250:100:125 kg NPK/ha.
- Biofertilizer inoculation can save up to 25% of chemical nitrogen requirement.
- Ratoon crop needs 25% more nitrogen than plant crop.
