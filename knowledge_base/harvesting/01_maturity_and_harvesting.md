# Sugarcane Maturity Indicators and Harvesting Practices

## Summary
Correct harvest timing is critical to sugar recovery: sugarcane should be harvested only when sucrose content reaches at least 16.5% with 85% juice purity. IISR documents the physical indicators of maturity, typical maturity windows for north and south India, and expected yield ranges, along with ratoon management practices that determine whether a good second crop follows harvest.

## Main Information
- Sugarcane matures within 10–12 months in north India and 12–16 months in south India, depending on the season of planting.
- The crop should be harvested when sucrose content reaches a minimum of 16.5% with 85% juice purity — since impurities in total dissolved solids are the key factor governing harvest timing, not simply calendar age.
- This maturity stage typically arrives during December–January when atmospheric temperature is around or below 20°C. Under high-temperature conditions, sucrose converts to glucose, resulting in poorer produce quality — reinforcing why cool, dry weather at harvest is preferred.
- **Visual/physical maturity indicators**: yellowing of leaves, emergence of arrows (flowering spikes), cane becoming brittle and breaking easily, a metallic sound when cane is struck, and swelling out of buds from the nodes.
- **Cane yield**: a good crop in north India yields about 80–100 tonnes/ha; in south India (particularly Adsali crop), yield can exceed 150 tonnes/ha.
- **Harvesting for good ratoon establishment**: good ratoon crops come from good plant crops harvested under favourable conditions. Autumn-planted cane harvested early in the crushing season gives better sprouting and ratoon yield. Harvesting should be done close to the ground level to promote better sprouting and survival of underground buds/tillers.
- Prior to harvest, **tying and wrapping** operations (typically done in August, when cane reaches about 2 m height) provide mechanical support against lodging: leaves are removed and canes bundled together, then clumps in adjacent rows are cross-tied. Green leaves should not be bundled together while tying.

## Important Recommendations
- Harvest decisions should be based on sucrose/purity thresholds (≥16.5% sucrose, ≥85% purity) rather than a fixed calendar date, since crop duration legitimately varies 10–16 months by region and season.
- Where possible, schedule harvest for the cooler December–January window to avoid the sucrose-to-glucose conversion that occurs under high ambient temperature, which degrades juice quality.
- Harvest as close to ground level as practical, since stubble left too high reduces ratoon sprouting quality in the subsequent crop — this is especially relevant for farmers planning multiple ratoon cycles.

## Key Facts
- Minimum harvest-readiness threshold: 16.5% sucrose content, 85% juice purity.
- Typical maturity window: 10–12 months (north India) to 12–16 months (south India).
- South Indian Adsali crop can yield over 150 t/ha versus 80–100 t/ha typical in north India.
