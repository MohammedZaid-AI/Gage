# Post-Harvest Management of Sugarcane: Preventing Sucrose Loss

## Summary
Sugarcane is a perishable commodity once cut: sucrose losses of 20–30% of total synthesized sugar can occur between harvest and milling due to staling, primarily from endogenous invertase enzyme activity and microbial bio-deterioration (mainly *Leuconostoc* species). The ICAR-Sugarcane Breeding Institute (SBI), Coimbatore, documents the biochemical basis of these losses and eleven practical measures to minimize them — directly relevant for farmers whose cane faces transport delays to the mill.

## Main Information
- Sucrose losses after harvest and during subsequent milling are one of the most serious problems in Indian sugar processing. Staling beyond 24 hours causes considerable loss in cane weight due to moisture loss, and reduction in juice sucrose content due to inversion.
- Documented cane weight loss ranges from 7.4% to 17.0%, and sugar recovery loss of about 2.0%, at different locations in India due to staling of cane for 96 hours.
- **Nature of post-harvest changes**: moisture loss of 1.5–2% for every 24 hours (weather-dependent); sucrose loss in juice of 0.1–0.2 units per 24 hours (particularly after 48 hours) due to inversion; increased juice acidity and dextran/gum formation; apparent fibre content increase due to moisture loss; increase in reducing sugars from sucrose inversion into glucose and fructose.
- **Two biochemical mechanisms drive deterioration**: (1) endogenous invertase enzyme, activated soon after harvest, most abundant in immature tissue; (2) bio-deterioration by *Leuconostoc mesenteroides* and *L. dextranicum* bacteria, which enter through cut ends within about an hour of cutting and convert sucrose into dextran polysaccharides, which further disrupts filtration, clarification, and crystallization during milling.
- Indian sugar mills lose on average about 10–15 kg of sugar per tonne of cane crushed due to the harvest-to-milling time lag, even under well-managed conditions — losses that escalate if crushing extends into May/June.
- Factors affecting the rate of deterioration: cane variety (genetic and morphological traits such as rind hardness and wax coating), weather (high temperature ~40°C and low humidity 25–35% accelerate deterioration), crop maturity (immature or over-mature cane deteriorates faster), and whether cane is green or burnt (delaying harvest of burnt standing cane beyond 24 hours causes marked sugar-yield loss).
- Mechanically harvested (chopped) cane deteriorates faster than hand-cut whole cane due to greater surface exposure; in Queensland, Australia, chopped-harvester cane showed 6–11% CCS loss versus 1–2% loss for stored whole cane.

## Important Recommendations (11 measures to minimize stale-cane loss)
1. Use varieties known to resist post-harvest deterioration in areas where transport delay is likely.
2. Avoid harvesting immature or over-mature cane.
3. Arrange quicker transport specifically for varieties identified as more susceptible to deterioration.
4. Keep harvested cane under shade during hot weather.
5. Cover harvested cane with trash and periodically sprinkle water to keep it moist.
6. Avoid topping (removing the leafy crown) if any crushing delay is anticipated, since topped cane deteriorates faster than cane with its crown intact.
7. Dip cut ends of cane in biocides such as polycide or bactrinol (100 ppm) — spraying stored cane with these can arrest deterioration for up to 120 hours.
8. Where feasible, apply pre-harvest chemical ripeners such as sodium metasilicate (2% solution, applied 3 days before harvest) to help maintain juice quality of stored cane for up to 6 days.
9. Minimize the time lag between harvesting and milling as the single most important control — this is more impactful than any individual chemical treatment.
10. Ensure "first cane in, first cane out" practice at collection yards to avoid unnecessary storage delays.
11. Transport fresh cane in small containers rather than large ones, since smaller containers are less prone to internal heat build-up and deterioration.

## Key Facts
- Sucrose loss window: deterioration accelerates markedly after 48 hours; staling for 96 hours can cause 7.4–17.0% cane weight loss and ~2.0% sugar recovery loss.
- Average Indian mill-level loss: 10–15 kg sugar per tonne of cane crushed, due to time lag alone.
- Moisture loss rate: 1.5–2% per 24 hours after cutting.
- Best-practice window for processing: within 24–48 hours of cutting, per multiple cross-referenced sources.
