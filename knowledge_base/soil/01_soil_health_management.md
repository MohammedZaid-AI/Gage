# Soil Health Management for Sugarcane

## Summary
Soil health for sugarcane cultivation is managed in India through two complementary channels: (1) technical soil-nutrient replenishment guidance from ICAR-IISR, and (2) the Government of India's Soil Health Card (SHC) Scheme, implemented at the state level (including by the Karnataka Department of Agriculture), which provides farmers with periodic soil testing and nutrient-specific recommendations.

## Main Information

### Soil nutrient replenishment (technical basis)
- A 100 t/ha sugarcane crop removes substantial soil nutrients (208 kg N, 53 kg P, 280 kg K, 30 kg S, plus micronutrients), meaning soil fertility must be actively replenished through fertilizer and organic matter rather than assumed to be self-sustaining.
- Application of well-decomposed FYM/compost at 10–15 t/ha, or composted press mud at 5 t/ha, is recommended as a baseline organic soil-health input.
- Resource Conservation Technology (RCT) practices recommended for sugarcane soils include: nitrogen-fixing (Azospirillum, Gluconacetobacter) and phosphate-solubilizing (Phosphobacteria) biofertilizers (see the Fertilizer document for the specific chemical-nitrogen-saving figure this delivers); trash mulching combined with drip irrigation (up to 30% water saving); and use of implements like the ratoon management device, sugarcane cutter-planter, trench opener, and power weeder for more effective soil and residue management.
- The **Sustainable Sugarcane Initiative (SSI)**, developed under an ICRISAT-WWF project, is a documented method combining reduced seed use, reduced water use, and optimized fertilizer/land use. Its core principles include: raising nursery from single-budded chips; transplanting young seedlings (25–35 days old); maintaining wide spacing (5×2 feet) in the main field; providing sufficient moisture while avoiding water inundation; encouraging organic nutrient and plant-protection methods; and intercropping for effective land utilization.

### Soil Health Card Scheme (government program)
- The Soil Health Card Scheme provides for periodic testing of soil and nutrient management recommendations, issuing soil health cards every 3 years for all landholdings, to capture fertility changes from plant uptake or other natural causes over time.
- As implemented at the district level in Karnataka (e.g., Bangalore Rural district), the scheme is explicitly linked to follow-up technical support: "small and marginal farmers need technical support to apply site specific fertilizer recommendations" based on the nutrient deficiencies identified in their soil health cards.
- Related state schemes distribute soil-health-supporting inputs on subsidy, including gypsum, micronutrients, biofertilizers, green manure seeds, and vermicompost.

## Important Recommendations
- Farmers should treat the Soil Health Card as the starting point for any fertilizer decision (rather than applying a blanket state-recommended dose), since it identifies field-specific nutrient deficiencies that the general zone-wise fertilizer recommendation cannot capture.
- Combine chemical fertilizer replenishment (matched to actual nutrient removal, see Fertilizer document) with organic inputs (FYM, press mud, biofertilizers) rather than relying on chemical fertilizer alone, both for cost reduction and for longer-term soil structure/fertility maintenance.
- For farmers seeking to reduce input costs while sustaining yield, the Sustainable Sugarcane Initiative (SSI) principles — particularly wide spacing and single-bud nursery transplanting — offer a documented alternative production system worth considering, especially where labour and seed cane costs are constraints.

## Key Facts
- Soil Health Cards are reissued every 3 years per landholding under the national scheme.
- Baseline organic input recommendation: FYM/compost 10–15 t/ha, or composted press mud 5 t/ha.
- SSI wide-spacing standard: 5×2 feet in the main field, using 25–35 day-old single-bud nursery seedlings.
