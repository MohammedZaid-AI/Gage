# Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY): Crop Insurance for Farmers

## Summary
Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY), launched in 2016 by the Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare, is India's flagship crop insurance scheme and the largest such scheme in the world by farmer applications. It provides financial protection against yield losses from natural calamities, pests, and diseases, with affordable, capped farmer premiums and the balance subsidized by central and state governments. As of its ninth anniversary (February 2025), the scheme has been extended through 2025-26 with a ₹69,515.71 crore budget.

## Main Information
- **Premium structure**: the maximum premium payable by the farmer is capped at 2% of the sum insured for Kharif food and oilseed crops, 1.5% for Rabi food and oilseed crops, and 5% for annual commercial/horticultural crops (sugarcane, being a commercial crop, generally falls in the higher premium-rate category, though the government subsidizes the remainder of the actuarial premium). The remaining premium above the farmer's capped share is subsidized by the government.
- **Risks covered**:
  1. *Yield losses (standing crop)*: coverage for non-preventable risks including natural fire and lightning, storm, hailstorm, cyclone, typhoon, tempest, hurricane, tornado, flood, inundation, landslide, drought, dry spells, and pest/disease attack.
  2. *Prevented sowing*: if adverse weather prevents planting, farmers can claim indemnity up to a maximum of 25% of the sum insured.
  3. *Post-harvest losses*: coverage on an individual-farm basis for crops in "cut and spread" condition, for up to 14 days (maximum) from harvesting.
  4. *Localised calamities*: individual-farm-basis coverage for hailstorm, landslide, and inundation affecting scattered farmlands within a notified area.
- **Eligibility**: all farmers, including sharecroppers and tenant farmers growing notified crops in notified areas, are eligible. The scheme is voluntary; non-loanee farmers' share of total coverage rose to 55% in 2023-24, reflecting growing voluntary uptake.
- **Technology integration**: PMFBY uses satellite imagery, drones, UAVs, and remote sensing for crop area estimation and yield-dispute resolution. The CCE-Agri App captures Crop Cutting Experiment (CCE) data for direct upload to the National Crop Insurance Portal (NCIP). From Kharif 2023, YES-TECH (Yield Estimation System Based on Technology) has been introduced to blend technology-based and manual yield estimates for loss assessment.
- **Claim timelines**: PMFBY aims to process claims within two months of harvest, to reduce farmers' risk of falling into debt traps while awaiting compensation.
- **Related scheme — RWBCIS**: the Restructured Weather Based Crop Insurance Scheme, introduced alongside PMFBY, uses a weather-index-based methodology (rather than yield-based) for calculating admissible claims.
- **Karnataka implementation**: PMFBY has been implemented in Karnataka since 2016-17 as "Karnataka Raitha Suraksha PMFBY," coordinated by the Karnataka Department of Agriculture alongside the central scheme.
- **Scale (national)**: since 2016, 29.19 crore farmer applications have been insured under PMFBY; more than ₹95,000 crore in claims have been paid against ₹17,000 crore of farmer-paid premium (as of the 2025 nine-year review) — illustrating the scheme's redistributive design in favour of farmers.

## Important Recommendations
- Farmers should verify whether sugarcane is a **notified crop** in their specific district for the relevant season, since PMFBY coverage operates on a notified-crop, notified-area basis set by the State Level Coordination Committee on Crop Insurance (SLCCCI) — coverage is not automatic for every crop everywhere.
- Because premium payment for PMFBY must be made via NEFT only (demand drafts, cheques, and offline enrolment are not accepted), farmers should ensure they have working bank account access and complete enrolment digitally before the seasonal deadline.
- Given the "cut and spread" post-harvest coverage window is capped at 14 days, farmers should coordinate harvest and transport timing carefully — this connects directly to the sucrose-loss urgency documented in the Post-Harvest Management file, since both insurance claims and sugar recovery deteriorate the longer cut cane remains unprocessed.

## Key Facts
- Farmer premium cap: 2% (Kharif), 1.5% (Rabi), 5% (commercial/horticultural crops — relevant to sugarcane).
- Prevented-sowing indemnity cap: up to 25% of sum insured.
- Post-harvest loss coverage window: up to 14 days from harvest ("cut and spread" condition).
- Total PMFBY budget approved for continuation through 2025-26: ₹69,515.71 crore.
- Non-loanee farmer share of total coverage (2023-24): 55%.
