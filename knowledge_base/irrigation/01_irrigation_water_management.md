# Sugarcane Irrigation Water Management

## Summary
Sugarcane is a long-duration, water-intensive crop requiring 1,500–2,500 mm of water over its growing cycle depending on climate. IISR documents phase-wise irrigation schedules, state-wise irrigation requirements, and three water-economizing techniques (skip-furrow, sprinkler, drip) that can cut water use by 30–95% while maintaining or improving productivity — highly relevant for Karnataka, which has one of the highest irrigation-water requirements among sugarcane-growing states in India.

## Main Information
- General guidance: irrigation water depth of 7–8 cm per application is recommended, with frequency varying by crop phase — approximately once every 7 days during germination (1–35 days after planting, DAP), once every 10 days during tillering (36–100 DAP), once every 7 days during grand growth (101–270 DAP), and once every 15 days during maturity (271 DAP to harvest), adjusted for rainfall.
- Subtropical regions typically need 6–8 irrigations across the season; tropical regions (including Karnataka) may need 20–36 irrigations due to climatic conditions, annual rainfall, planting season, crop duration, and soil type.
- **Karnataka-specific data**: 32 irrigations required per season; average yield 85.5 t/ha; irrigation water required to produce 1.0 kg of sugar is 266 kg — among the highest of all sugarcane states reported, reflecting Karnataka's tropical multi-irrigation cropping pattern.
- **Net irrigation water requirement by technique (Karnataka, mm/year)**: furrow irrigation 1,878 mm; skip furrow 1,408 mm; sprinkler 1,325 mm; drip 1,186 mm — showing drip irrigation can reduce net water requirement by roughly 37% compared to conventional furrow irrigation for the same crop water demand.

### Water-economizing methods
1. **Skip-furrow/alternate furrow irrigation**: one row is skipped and irrigation applied only to alternate furrows, saving 30–40% water without reducing cane productivity.
2. **Sprinkler irrigation**: most useful in the first ~5 months after planting (or after ratoon harvest); increases bud sprouting (up to 90%), aids decomposition of trash mulch, avoids soil compaction, and is well suited to undulating/steep land where levelling is not required. Delivers about 50–60% water economy. Installation cost: roughly ₹75,000–₹1.5 lakh.
3. **Drip irrigation**: water applied directly to the root zone via a tube/emitter network; achieves up to 95% application uniformity when properly designed, and delivers the highest water-use efficiency of the three methods.

## Important Recommendations
- Given Karnataka's high per-season irrigation requirement (32 irrigations, 266 kg water per kg sugar produced), farmers in the state should prioritize migration from furrow irrigation to drip irrigation, which the source data shows can lower net water requirement from ~1,878 mm/year to ~1,186 mm/year.
- During monsoon disruptions, adopt the contingency schedule appropriate to the delay: for a 2-week delayed monsoon onset, continue pre-monsoon irrigation intervals (8–10 days) with alternate-furrow application and trash mulching in ratoon; for longer delays (6–8 weeks), shift to a 10-day protective irrigation interval and watch for iron deficiency, which becomes likely under prolonged dry spells (foliar spray of 1% FeSO₄ + 0.5% ZnSO₄ + 2% urea is recommended in that case).
- In waterlogging or flood situations, avoid prolonged water stagnation; after drainage, apply urea at 60 kg/ha (or a 3% urea foliar spray if soil application is not feasible), and control sucking pests, stalk borer, and top borer, which increase after flooding.

## Key Facts
- Total seasonal water requirement: 1,500–2,500 mm, depending on climate.
- Karnataka irrigation requirement: 32 irrigations/season; 266 kg water per kg sugar produced.
- Skip-furrow irrigation: 30–40% water saving.
- Sprinkler irrigation: 50–60% water economy; drip irrigation: up to 95% application uniformity.
- Recommended irrigation depth per application: 7–8 cm.
