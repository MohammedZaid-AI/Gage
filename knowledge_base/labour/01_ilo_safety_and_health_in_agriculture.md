# Safety and Health in Agriculture: ILO Code of Practice

## Summary
The International Labour Organization (ILO) recognizes agriculture as one of the most hazardous economic sectors worldwide, employing roughly one billion workers (over a third of the global labour force) and accounting for a large share of occupational accidents and ill health. The ILO's Code of Practice on Safety and Health in Agriculture complements the Safety and Health in Agriculture Convention, 2001 (No. 184) and its accompanying Recommendation (No. 192), providing practical guidance for reducing occupational safety and health (OSH) risk in the sector.

## Main Information
- Agriculture is identified by the ILO as one of the three most hazardous sectors of economic activity (alongside construction and mining), based on fatality rates, occupational accidents, and work-related ill health.
- Agriculture is also the largest sector for female employment in many countries, particularly across Africa and Asia — directly relevant to sugarcane cultivation in India, where women perform a substantial share of manual field operations (weeding, planting, harvesting support).
- Agriculture accounts for approximately 70% of child labour worldwide, underscoring the sector's distinct occupational-safety and social-protection challenges compared to other industries.
- The Code of Practice provides guidance on strategies to address the range of OSH risks encountered in agriculture, aiming to prevent — as far as reasonably possible — accidents and diseases for all workers engaged in the sector, complementing binding international labour standards (Convention 184) with practical implementation guidance.
- Related ILO technical guidance referenced alongside this Code includes the *Technical and Ethical Guidelines for Workers' Health Surveillance* (Occupational Safety and Health Series No. 72) and further ILO guidance specifically addressing occupational safety and health in agriculture, on plantations, and in other rural sectors.

## Important Recommendations
- Any farm-labour safety guidance built into a farmer advisory system should explicitly address the elevated hazard profile of manual harvesting activities (a documented high-risk task in sugarcane specifically — see the companion document on sugarcane cutting hazards), consistent with ILO's classification of agriculture as a top-tier hazardous sector.
- Because agriculture is a major source of female employment, safety guidance and protective-equipment recommendations should be designed for and made accessible to women agricultural workers specifically, not assumed to be gender-neutral by default — this connects directly to the companion Women's Health document in this knowledge base.
- Given agriculture's high share of child labour globally, any advisory or safety-training content developed for farm labourers should reinforce the legal prohibition on employing children in hazardous agricultural tasks (see also India's constitutional protections under Article 24, referenced in the Women's Health document).

## Key Facts
- Global agricultural workforce: approximately 1 billion workers, over one-third of the global labour force.
- Agriculture accounts for approximately 70% of child labour worldwide.
- Governing international standard: ILO Safety and Health in Agriculture Convention, 2001 (No. 184) and Recommendation (No. 192).
