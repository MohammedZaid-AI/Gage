# ICAR Kharif Agro-Advisory 2025: Sugarcane (Current Season Practices)

## Summary
"ICAR Kharif Agro-Advisories for Farmers 2025" is ICAR's current, nationally coordinated season advisory (published May 2025), compiled jointly by ICAR institutes, State Agricultural Universities, and Krishi Vigyan Kendras (KVKs), covering all 28 states and 8 Union Territories. It supersedes older static package-of-practices documents for current-season operational guidance. The sugarcane section reproduced here is drawn from the Bihar state chapter (the sugarcane-specific chapter retrieved in this pass); Karnataka has its own dedicated chapter (pp. 110–119) in the same publication which should be sourced directly for state-specific detail in a future collection pass.

## Main Information
- This is a **live, current-season document** (2025 Kharif cycle, June–October), explicitly designed to reflect the latest research and field-level insights from ICAR institutes, Agricultural Universities, and KVKs, as stated in its own preface.
- **Sugarcane operational window covered**: from June onward, sugarcane enters early vegetative and grand growth phase for spring-planted (Feb–March) crop; late/autumn planting can continue until early July where irrigation or pre-monsoon rains allow.
- **Earthing-up**: recommended by mid-June for spring-planted crops, to promote root growth, prevent lodging, and enhance moisture conservation.
- **Trash mulching**: recommended in the same window to suppress weeds, conserve soil moisture, and support beneficial microbial activity.
- **Gap filling**: any gap filling with healthy setts should be completed early in June to maintain uniform crop stand.
- **Nitrogen top-dressing**: second split of nitrogen (approximately 60–75 kg N/ha) using urea should be applied before rains or irrigation, following the previously applied basal dose.
- **Zinc deficiency**: in zinc-deficient fields, apply 25 kg zinc sulphate/ha, or spray 0.5% ZnSO₄ solution on the standing crop.
- **Intercropping**: short-duration pulses (moong, urd) intercropped during early monsoon are recommended to improve soil fertility and farmer income.
- **Weeding/hoeing**: should be completed by July to avoid competition during the peak growth phase.
- **Pest surveillance from June onward**:
  - *Top borer*: common in this period; apply Chlorantraniliprole 0.4% GR @ 10 kg/ha in leaf whorls during early July.
  - *Pyrilla*: release of *Epiricania melanoleuca* parasitoids, or spray Acephate 75 SP @ 1 g/litre.
  - *Red rot and wilt*: monitor susceptible varieties; promptly remove and destroy infected clumps.
- **Drainage**: proper drainage must be maintained to prevent water stagnation during heavy monsoon rains.
- **Expected yield**: with good management, 60–80 t/ha by harvest in January–February (this is a Bihar-specific yield figure, lower than Karnataka's typical 85+ t/ha owing to differing agro-climatic conditions; see the separate Sugarcane Cultivation Overview document for Karnataka-specific yield data).

## Important Recommendations
- Because this is explicitly a rolling, season-specific advisory (not a static reference), any RAG system should treat it as time-bound guidance for the June–October 2025 Kharif window specifically, and should be refreshed with each year's new edition rather than treated as permanently valid.
- The Karnataka-specific chapter of this same 2025 publication (pages 110–119) should be sourced in a follow-up collection pass, since sugarcane cultivation timing, varieties, and pest pressure differ meaningfully between Bihar (subtropical, sub-optimal recovery) and Karnataka (tropical, high recovery) — the figures in this document should not be applied directly to Karnataka farmers without that state-specific chapter.
- The core seasonal operations sequence (earthing-up → gap-filling → mulching → nitrogen top-dress → weeding → pest surveillance, all within the June–July window) is a useful current cross-check against the older IISR 2017 package-of-practices guidance already in this knowledge base, and is broadly consistent with it — reinforcing that the 2017 technical thresholds (fertilizer doses, pest dosages) remain valid as underlying agronomic science even where the 2025 advisory adds current-season timing emphasis.
