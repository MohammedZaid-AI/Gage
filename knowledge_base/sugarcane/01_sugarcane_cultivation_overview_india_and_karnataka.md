# Sugarcane Cultivation in India: Agro-Climatic Overview (with Karnataka Focus)

## Summary
Sugarcane (*Saccharum* spp. hybrid) is India's principal commercial/industrial cash crop, cultivated on about 49.18 lakh hectares nationally. India is the world's second-largest producer of both sugarcane and sugar. Cultivation is organized into two broad agro-climatic regions — tropical and sub-tropical — with distinct climatic requirements, planting seasons, and productivity levels. Karnataka falls within the tropical/peninsular zone, where cane yields (85.5 t/ha in 2015-16 data) are markedly higher than the subtropical national average (69.4 t/ha), owing to long sunshine hours, cool nights, and favourable latitude for sugar accumulation.

## Main Information
- Sugarcane requires a mean temperature of 28–32°C for optimal growth; temperatures above 45°C reduce tillering, and below 20°C slow growth. Areas with minimum temperature under 5°C are unsuitable.
- The crop needs 75–120 cm of annual rainfall (if unirrigated) and a long growing season of 10–18 months. Ideal relative humidity is 70–85% during growth and 55–75% during ripening; below 50% humidity during growth is unsuitable.
- India has two broad sugarcane regions:
  - **Tropical region** (Maharashtra, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh, Kerala): ~45% of area, 55% of national production; productivity ~80 t/ha.
  - **Sub-tropical region** (Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Haryana, Punjab, Uttarakhand, West Bengal, Assam, Odisha, Rajasthan, and others): ~55% of area, 45% of production; productivity ~60 t/ha, constrained by a shorter growing season, floods/waterlogging, and higher pest/disease incidence.
- In Karnataka specifically (2015–16 data): area under sugarcane was 4.00 lakh hectares, production 342.00 lakh tonnes, and productivity 85.5 t/ha — among the highest in the country, close to Tamil Nadu's 105.0 t/ha.
- Karnataka's sugarcane research is coordinated through the AICRP on Sugarcane centre at ZARS, V.C. Farm, Mandya, under the University of Agricultural Sciences, Bangalore (UAS-B), in coordination with ICAR-IISR, Lucknow.
- Planting seasons in the Peninsular Zone (which includes Karnataka): main planting January–February; Adsali (long-duration, 16–18 month crop) planting July–August, which passes through only one summer and gives higher yield and sugar recovery but is declining due to reduced irrigation water availability.
- Recommended early-maturing varieties for Karnataka (per AICRP zonal recommendations): CoC 671, Co 94012, Co 92005, CoSnk 05103, SNK 09211. Recommended mid-late varieties: Co 86032, CoM 0265, CoSnk 03632, CoSnk 05104, SNK 07680. Co 86032 alone occupies roughly 53% of Karnataka's early/mid-late varietal share in the source data, prized for high yield (~120 t/ha), high sucrose, good ratooning ability, and tolerance of drought and saline conditions.
- Sugarcane is a multi-product crop: besides sugar, gur (jaggery) and khandsari, it supplies raw material to over 25 industries (alcohol, paper, chemicals, cattle feed) and is used for ethanol production and co-generation of electricity at sugar mills.

## Important Recommendations
- Farmers should select varieties matched to their specific agro-climatic zone and planting season (early vs mid-late vs late) rather than using a single variety across all conditions, to balance cane tonnage with sugar recovery.
- In Karnataka and other Adsali-planting tropical states, farmers with adequate water availability can consider Adsali (July–August) planting for higher yield and sugar recovery, provided irrigation water is not a limiting constraint.
- Because sugarcane occupies land for 12–18 months, crop diversification through intercropping or sequential cropping (e.g., paddy–sugarcane–ratoon–finger millet/sesamum/blackgram systems, which are documented for Karnataka's peninsular zone) is recommended to generate mid-season income and reduce monoculture risk.

## Key Facts
- National sugarcane area (2015-16): 49.18 lakh hectares; production: 3,414.25 lakh tonnes; average productivity: 69.4 t/ha.
- Karnataka productivity (2015-16): 85.5 t/ha, versus 40.64–61.7 t/ha in subtropical Uttar Pradesh over the same period.
- Crop duration: 10–12 months (north India) to 12–16 months (south India), depending on season and variety group (early/mid-late/late).
- India produced 28.1 million tonnes of sugar in the 2014-15 marketing year and is the world's second-largest sugar producer.
