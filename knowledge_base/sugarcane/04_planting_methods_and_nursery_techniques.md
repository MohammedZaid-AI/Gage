# Sugarcane Planting Methods and Advanced Nursery Techniques

## Summary
IISR documents five conventional field-planting methods (flat bed, ridge-and-furrow, trench, FIRB, and ring pit) suited to different rainfall and drainage conditions, plus four advanced seed-multiplication/nursery techniques (tissue culture, spaced transplanting, cane node/poly bag, and bud chip) that improve germination, reduce seed cane requirement, and produce disease-free planting material.

## Main Information

### Conventional field planting methods
1. **Flat bed planting** — the simplest, cheapest method, used in low-rainfall areas of northern India and parts of Maharashtra. Shallow furrows (8–10 cm deep) are opened 75–90 cm apart; setts placed end-to-end (3-budded sett per 30 cm of furrow); covered with 5–7 cm soil.
2. **Ridge and furrow method** — used in moderate-rainfall areas with drainage concerns. 'V'-shaped furrows ~90 cm apart, 20–25 cm deep; setts placed horizontally end-to-end. Two earthing-up operations transform furrows into ridges over the season.
3. **Trench method** — used in coastal areas and regions with strong winds/lodging risk. Trenches 75–90 cm apart, ~30 cm deep; fertilizer mixed into trench soil; setts drenched with chlorpyriphos 20 EC (1 kg a.i./ha) against soil pests before planting.
4. **Furrow Irrigated Raised Bed (FIRB) technique** — wheat sown on raised beds in November; sugarcane planted in furrows (80–85 cm apart) in February. Gives ~30% higher cane yield versus sequential wheat-sugarcane cropping, with ~20% water saving since irrigation is applied only in furrows.
5. **Ring pit planting** — pits of 2.25 ft diameter, 1.25 ft depth, with 21 two-budded setts per pit, can give 1.5–2.0 times higher cane yield than conventional planting.

### Advanced seed multiplication/nursery techniques
- **Tissue culture**: enables rapid multiplication of high-yielding, disease-resistant varieties; conventional vegetative propagation requires large seed quantities and has a low multiplication rate (10-12 years from variety release to field-scale coverage); tissue culture can compress this multiplication timeline to about 5 years and produces disease-free plantlets from apical meristem tissue.
- **Spaced Transplanting (STP)**: raised nursery beds (3 ft wide) are prepared with FYM; single-bud setts are dipped in 0.2% Carbendazim, planted vertically, and treated with chlorpyriphos or imidacloprid against pests. Settlings are transplanted after 6–7 weeks, achieving 90–95% survival with careful management. Seed cane requirement drops to ~20 quintals/ha versus ~60 quintals/ha for normal planting.
- **Cane Node/Poly Bag Technique**: single-bud setts dipped in 0.1% Carbendazim, planted in perforated poly bags (12×8 cm) filled with a 1:2:5 sand:soil:FYM mix; used for gap-filling with very low field mortality (~5%) since the root system is undamaged at transplanting.
- **Bud Chip Technique**: buds with a portion of nodal tissue are extracted using a bud-chipping machine (developed at Sugarcane Breeding Institute, Coimbatore); only 15 quintals of bud chips are needed per hectare (versus 60+ quintals of whole cane), with the remaining stalk sent for milling. Bud chips are treated with chlorpyriphos + carbendazim, incubated overnight, then raised in trays for 6–8 weeks before transplanting.

## Important Recommendations
- Match the field-planting method to local rainfall/drainage conditions: flat-bed for low-rainfall/well-drained areas, ridge-and-furrow or trench for higher rainfall/drainage-risk or wind-prone (coastal) areas.
- Where seed cane is expensive or scarce, adopt STP, cane-node, or bud-chip nursery techniques to cut seed requirement by up to two-thirds while improving germination uniformity and disease-free status of planting material.
- The FIRB technique is specifically recommended for water-scarce regions practising wheat-sugarcane rotation, as it saves roughly 20% irrigation water without reducing wheat yield.

## Key Facts
- Ring pit planting can yield 1.5–2.0× the cane yield of conventional flat planting.
- STP and cane-node techniques cut seed cane requirement from ~60 quintals/ha to ~15–20 quintals/ha.
- FIRB technique: ~30% higher cane yield than sequential cropping, with ~20% water saving.
- Settling survival rate after transplanting with careful STP management: 90–95%.
