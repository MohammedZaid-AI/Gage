# Nutrient Deficiency Management in Sugarcane (Iron Chlorosis and Micronutrients)

## Summary
Iron chlorosis is the most widely occurring nutritional deficiency in sugarcane, especially on calcareous soils, and worsens in successive ratoon crops. IISR documents six treatment options for chlorosis management and identifies the broader set of micronutrients (B, Cu, Fe, S, Mg, Mn, Mo, Zn) affecting cane growth, along with critical soil deficiency thresholds.

## Main Information
- Iron chlorosis has been observed in nearly all sugarcane-growing states of India, particularly Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, and Bihar; it is most severe on calcareous soils and tends to intensify with successive ratoons.
- **Six documented treatments for lime-induced iron chlorosis:**
  1. Foliar spray of 2% FeSO₄ solution combined with 0.5% MnSO₄ and 2% urea, applied two to three times.
  2. Soil application of FeSO₄ at 25 kg/ha.
  3. Soil application of press mud (5 t/ha) combined with iron pyrite (2 t/ha).
  4. Soil application of farm yard manure (25 t/ha) plus foliar application of 1.5% FeSO₄ with 1% urea at weekly intervals, and 1% ZnSO₄ at monthly intervals.
  5. Soil application of elemental sulphur at 30–40 kg/ha, applied 3 weeks before planting.
  6. Foliar spray of Fe-EDTA (50 ppm solution).
- Critical soil deficiency thresholds identified in the source: Fe (non-calcareous soil) below 4.2 ppm; Fe (calcareous soil) below 6.3 ppm; Zn (loamy soils) below 1.2 ppm; Zn (clay soils) below 2.0 ppm; Mn below 2.0 ppm; Cu below 1.2 ppm; hot-water-soluble Boron below 0.44 ppm.
- Beyond iron, cane growth and development are also affected by boron, copper, sulphur, magnesium, manganese, molybdenum, and zinc deficiencies; while needed in small quantities, these are essential where soil deficiencies exist. Calcareous soils in particular require attention to zinc and sulphur status.
- Where multiple micronutrient deficiencies exist, a combined basal application of 25 kg Ferrous Sulphate, 20 kg Zinc Sulphate, 10 kg Manganese Sulphate, 10 kg Copper Sulphate, 2.5 kg Sodium Molybdate, and 5 kg Borax per hectare, mixed with well-decomposed manure or compost, is advocated.
- Under drought conditions, contingency guidance also recommends a foliar spray of 1% FeSO₄ + 0.5% ZnSO₄ + 2% urea when iron deficiency is expected due to prolonged dry spells (6–8 weeks delayed monsoon).

## Important Recommendations
- Farmers on calcareous or high-pH soils (common in parts of Karnataka's black-soil tracts) should watch for chlorosis symptoms (interveinal yellowing of younger leaves) especially in ratoon crops, and apply combined foliar + soil iron treatment rather than a single application, since the source material documents six distinct combined strategies rather than one standalone fix.
- Soil testing against the documented critical thresholds (e.g., Fe below 4.2–6.3 ppm depending on soil type) should guide whether a general basal micronutrient mix or a targeted single-nutrient correction is warranted.
- During drought-related contingency management, proactively apply the FeSO₄+ZnSO₄+urea foliar spray rather than waiting for visible chlorosis symptoms, since iron deficiency is a known secondary effect of extended dry spells.

## Key Facts
- Critical Fe threshold: 4.2 ppm (non-calcareous soil) / 6.3 ppm (calcareous soil).
- Critical Zn threshold: 1.2 ppm (loamy soils) / 2.0 ppm (clay soils).
- Standard chlorosis foliar treatment: 2% FeSO₄ + 0.5% MnSO₄ + 2% urea, 2–3 applications.
- Combined micronutrient basal mix: 25 kg FeSO₄ + 20 kg ZnSO₄ + 10 kg MnSO₄ + 10 kg CuSO₄ + 2.5 kg Sodium Molybdate + 5 kg Borax per hectare.
