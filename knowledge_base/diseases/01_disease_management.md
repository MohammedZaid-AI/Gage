# Sugarcane Disease Management

## Summary
Sugarcane in India is affected by several economically damaging diseases — red rot, smut, wilt, grassy shoot disease, ratoon stunting disease, yellow leaf disease, pokkah boeng, and rust. IISR documents the causal organism and specific management approach for each, with an overarching emphasis on resistant varieties, sett/seed health, and hot-water/heat treatment as the primary defence.

## Main Information
- **Red Rot** (fungus: *Colletotrichum falcatum*): the most serious sugarcane disease in India. The most effective control is planting resistant varieties, combined with healthy cultural practices and crop rotation.
- **Smut** (fungus: *Sporisorium scitamineum*): managed through resistant varieties, seed selection and roguing of infected clumps, hot water treatment (52°C for 2 hours), and/or sett dipping in 0.2% Bavistin (carbendazim) for half an hour before planting.
- **Wilt** (fungus: *Fusarium sacchari*): seed material should be dipped in 0.2% Bavistin before planting; crop rotation with coriander or mustard as a companion crop is recommended; soil treatment with Borax at 15 kg/ha is beneficial; Trichoderma culture (10 kg/ha, CFU 10⁷ per g) is beneficial; roguing of infected clumps should be practiced.
- **Grassy Shoot Disease** (caused by a Phytoplasma): avoid selecting setts from diseased areas; rogue infected clumps; hot water treatment at 52°C for 2 hours is effective; the aphid vector should be controlled by insecticide spraying to prevent secondary infection.
- **Ratoon Stunting Disease** (bacterium: *Leifsonia xyli* subsp. *xyli*): planting healthy, disease-free seed cane is the key control measure. Planting material can be produced via meristem tip tissue culture, or via hot-water treatment: immersion of cuttings in running water at ambient temperature for 24–48 hours, followed by immersion in water at 50°C for 2–3 hours, to eliminate the bacterium before establishing seed nurseries.
- **Yellow Leaf Disease** (viral): the meristem culture technique is the most widely used method for virus elimination in apical shoot tissue; the source notes there is no effective direct control measure once the virus is present, underscoring the importance of using virus-free planting material.
- **Pokkah Boeng** (fungus: *Fusarium moniliforme*): spraying 0.2% Bavistin, 0.2% Blitox-50, or 0.2% Copper oxychloride are the most effective fungicide options.
- **Sugarcane rust** (fungus: *Puccinia melanocephala*): Propineb (0.25%) and Mancozeb (0.20%) are effective; should be sprayed on foliage just after rust pustules appear, three times at 15-day intervals.

## Important Recommendations
- Because most of these diseases (red rot, smut, wilt, grassy shoot, ratoon stunting, yellow leaf) are seed/sett-transmitted, the single highest-leverage preventive action for farmers is sourcing certified disease-free planting material (ideally tissue-culture or nursery-derived, per the Planting Methods document) rather than relying on curative treatment after symptoms appear.
- Hot water treatment at 50–52°C for 2–3 hours is a recurring, low-cost control measure across multiple diseases (smut, grassy shoot, ratoon stunting) and should be a standard nursery practice before establishing new seed material.
- For fungal foliar diseases (pokkah boeng, rust), timely fungicide spraying at first symptom appearance, repeated at 15-day intervals, is more effective than a single late-stage application.

## Key Facts
- Red rot is caused by *Colletotrichum falcatum*; resistant varieties are the primary control method — no curative chemical treatment is documented as reliably effective once infection is established.
- Standard hot-water/heat sett treatment across multiple diseases: 50–52°C for 2 hours.
- Ratoon stunting disease hot-water protocol: 24–48 hours ambient immersion, then 50°C for 2–3 hours.
- Rust/pokkah boeng fungicide spray interval: every 15 days, up to three applications.
